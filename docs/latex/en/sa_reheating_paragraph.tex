\paragraph{Reheating.}
A geometrically cooled SA eventually degenerates into greedy descent: once
$T$ is nearly zero the Metropolis rule rejects virtually every worsening
move, and the search freezes in the current basin of attraction. Our
implementation counters this with a \emph{reheating} mechanism driven by a
patience counter. At the end of every temperature level (a Markov chain of
$L = n^2$ evaluations), the incumbent $s^{*}$ is compared against its value
before the level: if it did not improve, a counter $u$ of consecutive
non-improving levels increases, and it resets to zero otherwise. When
$u \geq P$ (patience $P = 10$ levels), the temperature is restored to
$T \leftarrow \rho\, T_0$ with $\rho = 0.5$ --- half of the instance-aware
initial temperature --- and $u$ resets; the incumbent is always preserved,
so reheating re-enables uphill moves without discarding the best solution
found. Reheating also provides the practical stopping criterion of the
algorithm: at each reheat, the incumbent is compared against its value at
the previous reheat, and a second counter $r$ of consecutive
\emph{stale} reheats (reheats that produced no global improvement)
increases or resets accordingly. The run stops when $r \geq R$ (with
$R = 10$, calibrated by grid search over $\{3, 5, 10\}$), when the
classical condition $T \leq T_{\min}$ is met, or when the evaluation/time
budget is exhausted, whichever comes first. Intuitively, the pair $(P, R)$
grants the search up to $R$ ``second chances'', each consisting of $P$
levels of renewed exploration at temperature $\rho\, T_0$; the calibration
preferred the most patient setting, indicating that on CARP landscapes
late reheats still pay off within the budget.
